## 1. Earn Attention Before You Ask For Money

Having somebody's contact does not mean you have their attention.

You can have thousands of contacts and still get weak views because people already expect another product photo and price.

Before you post, ask:

**Why should the right customer tap my next status?**

One simple thing you can test is opening with a curiosity-based text connected to a problem your customer already cares about.

If you sell skincare, compare these two openings:

“Face cream available: ₦12,500.”

And:

“If your face becomes oily every afternoon, your cream may not be the real problem.”

The first one demands attention. The second one earns it.

Then pay off the curiosity. Explain the problem. Give useful information. Show how your product fits naturally.

Also, stop overwhelming people. If somebody opens your Status and sees fifty lines, they may decide they do not have the strength before watching the first one.

Test fewer, more intentional posts. Learn when your buyers are active. Make each post earn the next tap.

## 2. Enter The Conversation Already In Your Customer's Head

Your customer does not wake up thinking, “What is this business going to advertise to me today?”

They are thinking about themselves: their money, appearance, children, work, fears and desires.

Your content should meet them there.

I call this customer-intent content.

If you sell clothes, instead of posting only “New stock available,” you might begin with:

“Three small mistakes that can make an expensive outfit look cheap.”

Now you have entered a conversation the buyer may already care about. You can teach something useful, show examples and naturally connect the lesson to what you sell.

So stop asking only, “How can I advertise my product today?”

Ask, **“What does my customer already want to understand, avoid or achieve today?”**

When your Status gives people useful answers, they have a reason to keep watching even before they are ready to buy.

## 3. Give People Enough Evidence To Trust You

Attention gets somebody to watch. It does not automatically make them comfortable enough to pay.

Your next question is:

**If a stranger checks my business today, what gives them confidence that I am real, capable and safe to buy from?**

Look at your WhatsApp Business profile. Can somebody immediately understand what you sell? Is the information complete? Do you show genuine feedback, clear demonstrations and real results where you have them?

Teach what you know. Answer common questions. Show how the product works. Explain your process. Use proof you can verify.

Trust also appears in small conversations.

Instead of “Wait, I am coming,” say, “Let me confirm that for you.”

Instead of “We don't have it,” say, “That option is sold out, but I can recommend a similar one or tell you when the exact one returns.”

You are not pretending. You are reducing uncertainty and communicating like a professional.

## 4. Sell The Change, Not Only The Product

Views do not pay you. People buy when they believe your offer can move them from where they are to where they want to be.

So when somebody is interested, understand four things:

What is happening now?

What do they want instead?

Why does that change matter now?

What will it cost them in money, time, comfort or opportunity if they do nothing?

Imagine two people asking about the same wig.

One simply wants an affordable option. The other has a wedding on Saturday and wants to feel confident in every photograph.

Same wig. Different reason to buy.

For the second person, you are not only selling hair. You are helping her achieve how she wants to look and feel on an important day.

Do not only describe what your product is. Help the right customer understand the change it can help create.

## 5. Stop Losing People After They Show Interest

Someone asks, “How much?”

You send the price.

Silence.

It is easy to conclude, “They do not have money.” But silence can mean many things.

They may be comparing options. They may not trust you yet. They may not understand the value. They may have an objection they have not said. Or they may simply have become busy.

Respond quickly when you can, but do not throw only a price at them. Understand what they need. Connect the offer to that need. Answer the questions that make buying feel risky:

Can I trust you?

Will this work for me?

Is it worth the money?

When will I receive it?

Why should I choose this option?

And if they go quiet, follow up, but give them a reason to reopen the conversation.

Share useful information. Answer an objection. Show relevant proof. Remind them of the result they told you they wanted.

Do not send “Hello ma” five times. And do not assume every silence is rejection.
