So here are 5 things you can start changing.

---

# 1. GET THEIR ATTENTION FIRST

Before somebody buys, they first have to pay attention.

And having somebody's contact doesn't mean you have their attention.

You can have plenty contacts and still have very little views.

So every time you're posting, ask yourself:

**"Why should this person click my next status?"**

One thing you can test is starting your status with text.

But the text should give me a reason to continue.

Not just:

"Good morning guys."

Use something that creates curiosity.

Something surprising.

Something connected to a problem your customer already cares about.

Imagine someone selling skincare starts with:

> "If your face becomes oily every afternoon, your cream might not actually be the problem."

If I'm struggling with that, I want to see the next status.

Compare that with:

> "Face cream available — ₦12,500."

One is asking for attention.

The other has earned attention.

And don't overwhelm people.

Some of you, your WhatsApp status looks like waist bead.

Plenty, plenty, plenty.

Someone opens your status, sees all those lines and thinks:

**"I don't have strength for this."**

You can test fewer, more intentional posts.

Maybe 5 or 6 strong posts instead of dumping everything you sell.

You can also test posting at times when your audience is active and topping it up later.

The important thing isn't that everybody must post at exactly 5 PM.

The important thing is:

**Understand when your people are active and give them a reason to keep watching.**

---

# 2. STOP MAKING YOUR CONTENT ALL ABOUT YOU

Your customer doesn't wake up thinking:

**"What is this business going to sell me today?"**

They're thinking about themselves.

Their money.

Their problems.

Their appearance.

Their children.

Their business.

Their job.

Their desires.

So your content should enter the conversation already happening inside their head.

That's what I call **customer-intent content.**

If you're selling clothes, instead of every status being:

> "New stock available."

You could post:

> "3 things that can make an expensive outfit look cheap."

Now you've entered something the customer might care about.

Teach them something.

Show examples.

Make them curious.

And then your product can naturally become part of that conversation.

Instead of constantly asking:

**"How can I advertise my product today?"**

start asking:

**"What does my customer already care about today?"**

---

# 3. BUILD TRUST

Now let's say I watched your status.

I like the product.

I even have the money.

There's still another question:

**"Can I trust this person?"**

Look at your WhatsApp business profile.

Does it actually look credible?

Can someone immediately understand what you do?

If you've genuinely served hundreds of customers, are you showing that credibility?

Show real feedback.

Teach people.

Demonstrate your product.

Show how things work.

Answer common questions.

Show genuine results where you have them.

This is authority-based content.

You're giving me reasons to believe:

**"This person understands what they're selling."**

And trust also shows up in the little things.

How do you communicate?

Instead of:

> "Wait, I'm coming."

Try:

> "Let me confirm that for you."

Instead of:

> "I'll let you know."

Try:

> "I'll keep you updated."

Instead of simply:

> "We don't have it."

You could say:

> "That one is currently sold out, but there's a similar option I can recommend. If you want the exact one, I can also tell you when it's available."

And when something goes wrong, instead of immediately saying:

> "It's not our fault."

you can say:

> "Here's the best solution we can provide at this time."

You're not lying.

You're communicating professionally.

And those small things affect whether somebody feels comfortable giving you their money.

---

# 4. UNDERSTAND WHY PEOPLE ACTUALLY BUY

This is where we move from views to sales.

Because views alone don't pay you.

**People buy change.**

They're trying to move from their current situation to a desired situation.

So when somebody is interested in what you sell, you need to understand:

**What's motivating them?**

**What's their current situation?**

**What situation do they want?**

**What's the gap between those two situations?**

And:

**What happens if they do nothing?**

Let's say you sell wigs.

One person might just want something affordable.

Another person has a wedding on Saturday and wants to look amazing.

Same wig.

Different motivation.

For the second customer, you're not simply selling hair.

You're helping her achieve how she wants to **look and feel on Saturday.**

That's the change.

And once you understand that, your content becomes different.

Your offer becomes different.

Even the conversation you have with the customer becomes different.

Don't just describe what the product **is**.

Help people understand what it can help them **change**.

---

# 5. DON'T LOSE PEOPLE AFTER THEY SHOW INTEREST

Someone finally messages:

**"How much?"**

You send the price.

They disappear.

And immediately:

**"These people don't have money."**

Not necessarily.

Maybe they got distracted.

Maybe they're comparing you with somebody else.

Maybe there's an objection they haven't told you.

Maybe they don't trust you enough.

Maybe they don't see enough reason to act now.

Maybe life simply happened.

First, respond quickly where possible.

Then don't just throw information at people.

Understand what they're looking for.

And think about the objections they're likely to have before they even say them.

"Can I trust you?"

"Will this work for me?"

"Is it worth the money?"

"How long will delivery take?"

"Why should I buy this instead of the other option?"

Your content and sales process should answer these things.

And if somebody shows interest but doesn't buy:

**Follow up.**

But follow-up doesn't mean:

"Hello ma."

"Hello ma."

"Are you there ma?"

Give them a reason to reopen the conversation.

Maybe useful information.

Maybe proof.

Maybe an answer to an objection.

Maybe something new about the product.

Maybe a reminder connected to what they told you they wanted.

Don't assume every silence means rejection.
